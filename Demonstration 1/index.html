How much power do your appliances really use?

Everyday appliances such as televisions, ACs, etc. make up a large part of household electricity use in Australia. We explore and analyse how much energy is being consumpted, what is the cost and how to save your money!

Energy at home: the basics

  • Energy Rating Label

    Stars show efficiency

    Many appliances sold in Australia carry a star rating label. More stars means the appliance uses less energy than others of the same type and size.

  • Standby power

    Off is not always off

    Some appliances keep drawing a small amount of power while on standby. Across many devices and many hours, this can add up.

  • Running costs

    Watts × hours × price

    The cost of running an appliance depends on its power, how many hours a day it is used and the price you pay per kilowatt-hour.

Frequently asked questions

The Energy Rating Label is a star rating shown on appliances in Australia and New Zealand to help buyers compare energy efficiency.

Multiply the appliance's power in watts by the hours it is used each day, divide by 1,000 to get kilowatt-hours, then multiply by 365.

Standby power is usually small for a single device, but it runs around the clock and across many devices in a home.

Larger screens generally need more power, but screen technology and efficiency also make a difference. Try the calculator to compare models.
